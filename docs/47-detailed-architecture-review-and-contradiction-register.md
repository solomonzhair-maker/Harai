## 1. Executive result

**Disposition: G2 remains NOT APPROVED; G1 remains NOT PASSED.** The architecture proposals share a sound set of safety principles: preserve unknown outcomes, isolate provider-specific behavior, enforce tenant scope server-side, avoid duplicate financial effects, retain auditable financial history, and keep provider/legal/production authority separate from product intent. The review found several material design gaps and evidence blockers that must be resolved or explicitly accepted by the accountable decision-maker before a later implementation decision.

This review does **not** select a funds-flow model, approve a ledger/accounting model, establish provider capability, or authorize coding, provider contact, onboarding, merge, deployment, credentials, or live transactions.

### Classification used

- **Confirmed inconsistency:** two current artifacts make incompatible claims or statuses.
- **Design gap:** the principles are compatible, but a sufficiently precise rule or interface is missing.
- **Unverified assumption:** a design depends on a fact not established by current evidence.
- **External blocker:** closure requires provider, legal/compliance, finance, or other external authority/evidence.
- **Evidence gap:** a planned review/test/decision lacks the required recorded evidence.
- **Decision needed:** an accountable owner must choose among materially different options.

Severity is a planning priority, not a measured risk score. P0 findings block the affected approval or capability; P1 findings must be resolved or explicitly dispositioned before the relevant release scope is accepted.

## 3. Cross-document consistency conclusions

### 3.1 Principles that are aligned

The following are consistent across the reviewed proposals and should be preserved:

1. Provider acceptance, timeout, redirect or callback receipt alone is not universal proof of success.
2. UNKNOWN is a valid interim outcome; uncertain money-moving operations are not blindly resubmitted.
3. Provider-specific lifecycle behavior is isolated behind adapters and must be evidenced per method.
4. Refunds, reversals, returns, disputes and payouts are separate linked operations where applicable, not destructive edits to the original collection.
5. Financial history is intended to be append-only, with linked compensating corrections rather than silent mutation.
6. Tenant identity must be derived and enforced server-side; UI visibility is not authorization.
7. Proposed tests and role assignments are not actual test evidence or named appointments.
8. Funds-flow selection, provider permission, professional review, implementation authorization and production release remain separate decisions.

### 3.2 Important questions that are not classified as confirmed contradictions

- **Cancellation/expiry versus in-flight provider requests:** the documents already warn that in-flight attempts must still be reconciled. The remaining need is an atomic race/precedence contract, not a proven textual contradiction.
- **Immutable ledger versus reconciliation correction:** append-only entries and discrepancy resolution can coexist if corrections are compensating, linked and authorized. Account semantics and posting patterns still require finance review.
- **Provider-specific callback differences:** MTN and Airtel documentation need not promise identical delivery/authentication behavior. The adapter contract must preserve their differences rather than force false uniformity.
- **Comprehensive launch ambition versus blocked capabilities:** product intent may include collections, money-out and cards while individual capabilities remain disabled until permission and evidence exist. The distinction must remain visible in every merchant-facing claim and release gate.
- **Proposed deployment topology versus later technology selection:** a candidate is not a commitment; DOC-44 must record the eventual decision and rationale.

## 1. Authority and method

1. DOC-69 is a preliminary candidate inventory; it is not evidence of actual processing.
2. The comparison checks whether proposed domains and workflows in the referenced documents have a corresponding place in the candidate inventory and its evidence checklist.
3. A category can be mentioned indirectly in DOC-69 and still need a clearer row or cross-reference where it has materially different access, sensitivity, retention, or deletion behavior.
4. Findings recommend making the inventory complete enough to assess—not collecting every listed field. For each category, document whether it is in scope, not in scope, or still unverified.
5. No code, tests, production access, provider contact, credentials, live financial action, regulatory filing, merge, or deployment is authorized by this audit.

## 3. Cross-cutting consistency rules

The following rules should be preserved across DOC-69 and any later revised inventory:

1. **Separate raw evidence from normalized metadata.** Store only what is justified. Any raw provider message, attachment, communication body or diagnostic artifact requires its own purpose, access, retention and deletion decision.
2. **Separate operation layers.** A HARAI operation, provider attempt, callback receipt, ledger posting, settlement observation, case record and notification delivery are not interchangeable records and should not be treated as one lifecycle.
3. **Apply tenant scope to derived copies.** Search results, counts, caches, reports, exports, notifications, logs, temporary links and asynchronous jobs can disclose data even when primary record access is correct.
4. **Treat credentials differently from audit metadata.** Record that a credential exists and its state/scope; do not record the secret itself in this inventory, logs, case notes, examples or exports.
5. **Avoid collection by design-document accumulation.** A candidate category becomes in-scope only after a real workflow and necessity are established. The inventory is a minimization and evidence tool, not a feature backlog.
6. **No global retention shortcut.** Assign a retention rule per data class and copy/location, including legal hold, backup expiry, deletion propagation and restore behavior. Do not erase evidence required by an applicable obligation, and do not retain indefinitely merely because deletion is difficult.
7. **No assumed locations or providers.** Hosting regions, processors, support vendors, analytics SDKs, backup regions, cross-border transfers and provider data access remain UNVERIFIED until contract/configuration evidence exists.
8. **Privacy-rights and complaint workflows remain distinct.** Payment complaint, provider dispute, privacy complaint, data access/correction/deletion request, and security incident may be linked but must not be conflated.
9. **Sensitive payment data remains out of scope by default.** Do not collect or retain full card data or sensitive authentication data without a separately reviewed and authorized design. Confirm exact PCI scope with the eligible processor/acquirer and a qualified assessor.
10. **Draft controls are not verified controls.** A control statement in DOC-43 or DOC-64 is a design proposal until implementation and independent evidence are separately authorized and reviewed.

## 4. Recommended completion sequence

1. **Completed as a documentation pass:** DOC-69 now contains explicit candidate rows for all 12 gap categories and a row-level completion crosswalk. This does not verify that any category exists or is in scope; evidence and scope disposition remain outstanding.
2. Confirm which workflows and operation families are actually in the intended product scope; mark other categories OUT OF SCOPE with rationale rather than keeping them as implicit requirements.
3. For each in-scope category, identify the real UI/API/provider/source fields, system of record, data subjects, purpose, recipients, data roles, locations, access roles and copies.
4. Review retention, deletion, legal hold, backup propagation and restoration behavior with qualified privacy/legal and finance/accounting reviewers.
5. Review card-data boundaries, privileged access, telemetry, incident evidence, exports and cross-tenant derived artifacts with a qualified security reviewer.
6. Reconcile the resulting inventory with privacy notices, data-subject-rights handling, provider contracts, subprocessor records, incident response, complaint handling and applicable reporting duties.
7. Only after the evidence is complete should a revised inventory be considered for approval. Implementation and tests require separate explicit authorization and their own verification plan.
